# Mahjong Competition Rules (MCR) Beginner Guide

> **Scope:** International competition
> **Version:** MCR overview; verify the event rulebook
> **Summary:** An introduction to the international Chinese competition system and its eight-point minimum.

## Core format

MCR is a four-player competition system normally using 144 tiles. A standard
winning hand has four sets and a pair, although the scoring table also contains
special hands. Players draw and discard in turn, with standard sequence,
triplet, kong, and win claims.

## The eight-point requirement

A hand must normally contain at least eight points from valid scoring patterns
before Flower points. This is the central beginner hurdle: a structurally
complete hand is not automatically legal. Patterns may combine when the
rulebook permits, while exclusion and non-repetition principles prevent the
same feature being counted twice incorrectly.

## Scoring workflow

1. Confirm that the hand is structurally legal.
2. Identify the principal patterns.
3. Apply any implied, excluded, or non-repeatable relationships.
4. Confirm at least eight qualifying points.
5. Add Flower points only after legality is established.

## Settlement formula

Let `H` be the accepted hand-pattern points, excluding Flowers for the
eight-point legality check. In the common MCR settlement:

| Result | Discarder pays | Each other non-winner pays |
|---|---:|---:|
| Win on discard | `H + 8` | `8` |
| Self-draw | no single discarder | each pays `H + 8` |

Example: a legal 12-point hand wins on a discard. The discarder pays
`12 + 8 = 20`; each other player pays 8. If the same hand is self-drawn, each
opponent pays 20. Add Flower points at the scoring stage prescribed by the
event rules; do not use Flowers to reach the eight-point winning minimum.

## Beginner strategy

Plan a route to eight points early. Hands relying only on common low-value
features often finish below the minimum. Keep a short written scoring reference
near the table and practice recognizing a small group of compatible patterns
before trying to memorize the entire table.

Tournament organizers may publish clarifications and penalty procedures. Their
event documents take precedence over this introduction.
