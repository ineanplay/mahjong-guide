# Taiwanese 16-Tile Mahjong: Beginner Rules

> **Scope:** Taiwan
> **Version:** Common 16-tile framework; table scoring varies
> **Summary:** Learn the five-sets-and-a-pair structure used in Taiwanese 16-tile Mahjong.

## Main difference

Taiwanese Mahjong commonly gives each player 16 tiles, with the dealer holding
17 before the first discard. A normal winning hand therefore contains five sets
and one pair: 17 tiles after the winning draw or claim.

## Play

The draw-and-discard rhythm and chow, pung, kong, and win claims resemble other
four-player forms. Flowers are commonly used and replaced. Dealer continuation,
claim priority, ready declarations, and special replacement draws depend on the
table rules.

## Scoring

Taiwanese tables often score in tai, but the pattern list and minimum vary.
Self-draw, dealer status, consecutive dealership, Flowers, concealed hands,
all-pungs, and suit patterns may add value. Confirm the tai sheet before play.

## Beginner strategy

Because the hand requires five sets, flexibility matters longer. Keep connected
groups and avoid committing to a narrow pattern too early. Count all five sets
before declaring; players familiar with 13-tile Mahjong sometimes mistakenly
stop after forming only four.
