# Sichuan Bloody Rules Mahjong: Beginner Guide

> **Scope:** Sichuan, China
> **Version:** Common Bloody Battle framework; scoring varies
> **Summary:** Learn the missing-suit declaration, suit exchange, and continue-after-win format.

## Tile set and distinctive rules

Sichuan Bloody Rules commonly uses only the 108 suited tiles, without Winds,
Dragons, or Flowers. Before normal play, many tables exchange three tiles and
then declare a missing suit (dingque). A player normally must discard tiles of
that suit before using other suits freely and cannot win while holding it.

## Bloody Battle

The first winner leaves active play, but the hand continues for the remaining
players until all but one have won or the wall ends. This creates several
settlements within one deal and changes defense after each winner exits.

## Calls and scoring

Pungs, kongs, self-draw, and discard wins follow the chosen rule sheet. Chow is
commonly unavailable. Kong payments, root counts, capped multipliers, ready-hand
settlement, and penalties for holding the missing suit vary.

## Beginner strategy

Choose the missing suit by considering both tile count and connectivity. Remove
that suit efficiently, but watch which suits opponents eliminate. Fast simple
hands can be valuable because play continues after early wins. Confirm the
table's exchange direction and scoring cap before starting.
