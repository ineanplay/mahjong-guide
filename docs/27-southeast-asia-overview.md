# Southeast Asian Mahjong: Regional Overview

> **Scope:** Southeast Asia
> **Version:** Comparison of community rule families
> **Summary:** Compare documented Singaporean, Malaysian, Vietnamese, Filipino, and imported community rules across Southeast Asia.

## Scope and reliability

This page describes a regional rule family, not a government or nationwide standard. A city, province, or country label can cover several club, household, and platform variants.

## Commonly documented features

- Southeast Asia contains several distinct Mahjong traditions rather than one regional standard.
- Singapore and Malaysian tables are known for local bonus tiles and scoring; Vietnamese sets may add special tiles; Filipino play often uses a 16-tile hand; other communities may follow Hong Kong, Taiwanese, Chinese regional, or Japanese rules.
- Country of play alone does not identify the rules. Language, migration history, club practice, and available tile set often matter more.

## Confirm before the first deal

- Named ruleset and community
- Hand size
- Full tile inventory
- Bonus-tile actions
- Written scoring and settlement

## Beginner approach

Start with the ordinary draw-and-discard rhythm and four-sets-and-a-pair structure unless the host states otherwise. Ask for one legal winning example and one complete settlement example. Keep local terms beside their definitions, and never declare a win solely because a different app or regional guide accepts the same tile shape.
