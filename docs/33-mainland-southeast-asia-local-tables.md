# Mahjong in Myanmar, Laos, and Cambodia: Local-Table Guide

> **Scope:** Myanmar, Laos, and Cambodia
> **Version:** Orientation only; no unified cross-country ruleset
> **Summary:** A cautious guide for identifying local Chinese-community and imported Mahjong rules where documentation is limited.

## Scope and reliability

This page describes a regional rule family, not a government or nationwide standard. A city, province, or country label can cover several club, household, and platform variants.

## Commonly documented features

- Available general sources do not support declaring a single national Mahjong ruleset for Myanmar, Laos, or Cambodia.
- Local play may come from Chinese regional communities, neighboring Southeast Asian traditions, Hong Kong or Taiwanese teaching, clubs, or digital platforms.
- When documentation is limited, record the host's rules with examples instead of assigning a country label to an unsupported scoring system.

## Confirm before the first deal

- Community and source of rules
- Player and hand count
- Tile inventory
- Winning and calling rules
- Worked scoring example

## Beginner approach

Start with the ordinary draw-and-discard rhythm and four-sets-and-a-pair structure unless the host states otherwise. Ask for one legal winning example and one complete settlement example. Keep local terms beside their definitions, and never declare a win solely because a different app or regional guide accepts the same tile shape.
