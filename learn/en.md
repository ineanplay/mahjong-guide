# Mahjong: complete a first teaching session

[English](en.md) · [简体中文](zh.md) · [Türkçe](tr.md) · [Deutsch](de.md)

This lesson connects tile recognition, turns, a complete example and scoring. Start with the explicitly labeled teaching exercise, then move to your chosen rules. The exercise is not riichi, Hong Kong, MCR or American Mahjong.

## 1. Recognize the tiles

Characters, Bamboo and Dots each contain numbers 1-9 in four copies: 108 suited tiles. Four copies of each wind and dragon bring the common core to 136. Flowers, animals, wild tiles and removed tiles depend on the game; a box's total count alone does not identify the rules.

A sequence is three consecutive numbers of one suit, such as Dots 3-4-5. A triplet is three identical tiles, such as three Bamboo 2. A pair is two identical tiles. Numbers do not wrap from 9 to 1, and winds do not form numerical sequences.

## 2. Make one complete structure

| Group | Tiles | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Sequence 1 | Characters 2-3-4 | 3 |
| Sequence 2 | Characters 6-7-8 | 3 |
| Sequence 3 | Dots 3-4-5 | 3 |
| Triplet | Bamboo 2, 2, 2 | 3 |
| Pair | Bamboo 7, 7 | 2 |
| Total | Four groups and one pair | **14** |

Remove one Bamboo 7: 13 tiles remain. Another Bamboo 7 completes the structure. Regular rules also check yaku, fan, tai, minimum points or other restrictions. A complete structure is not automatically a legal win in every game.

## 3. A complete ten-minute teaching exercise

Practice drawing, discarding and structure with 108 suited tiles and four players. Omit honors, flowers and wild tiles. Initially use no chow, pung, kong or win on a discard: the last tile must be self-drawn to complete four groups and a pair. These are teaching agreements, not a regional ruleset.

1. Manually mix the tiles face down. Each player takes 13; choose a starter who takes one extra. The remainder is the face-down drawing supply.
2. The starter has 14, checks for completion and otherwise discards one, retaining 13.
3. Continue anticlockwise: draw one, check for completion, otherwise discard one.
4. A finisher shows all 14. Others check each group: suit, consecutive or identical tiles, pair, and no reuse of a physical tile.
5. Finisher +1, everyone else 0; no score when the supply runs out without a finish. Play four hands, changing the starter each time. Highest total wins; ties remain ties. These are learning points, not money.

Write these agreements on page 1 of the print pack. Four beginners can now complete a defined practice session without opening an external website.

## 4. Walk through a turn

Hold the 13-tile example with one Bamboo 7 removed. Draw Bamboo 7 and the four-group-and-pair check succeeds, ending this exercise's hand. Draw an unhelpful Characters 9 instead and you can discard it, retaining 13. Do not discard before drawing or draw twice in an ordinary turn.

## 5. Add claims after learning them

In common four-player rules, chow normally uses the previous player's discard to form a sequence; pung completes three identical tiles; kong uses four and follows a declaration and replacement-draw procedure. After chow or pung, usually discard without another ordinary draw. Claim priority and multiple wins must follow the chosen system. This introduces the concepts; it does not add them to the exercise above.

## 6. Choose regular rules

| Game | Next rules to learn |
|---|---|
| Japanese riichi | Yaku, concealed hands, furiten, dora, han/fu and payments; dora alone is not a yaku |
| Hong Kong | Minimum fan, flowers, payment table and limits |
| MCR | Scoring patterns, combination restrictions, minimum and flowers |
| Taiwanese 16-tile | Larger hand, five groups and a pair, tai and dealer continuation |
| American Mah Jongg | League and authorized annual card, exchange procedure and joker limits; not every hand uses four groups and a pair |

Learn one system before mixing terminology. The reference library provides further background, with English references labeled as English. They are not a prerequisite for this teaching session. Commercial annual cards are not reproduced here.

## 7. Record and review

If four hands finish A, C, C, nobody, the final totals are A=1, B=0, C=2, D=0. Enter changes in each hand row, then add them in the final row; do not sum running balances repeatedly.

Review: Did tile counts stay correct? Did anyone combine different suits into a sequence? Did anyone confuse a complete shape with a legal win in every system? Then learn the chosen rules one element at a time.
